12-Week, Twice-Weekly Strength & Hypertrophy Plan

Goal, assumptions, and schedule

  • Goal: general strength and hypertrophy in sessions capped at about 75 minutes.
  • Trainee: healthy intermediate with consistent recent training and sound basic technique; no current pain, injury, or medical restriction reported.
  • Equipment: standard commercial gym. Choose loads by the prescribed RIR, not by ego or fixed percentages.
  • Schedule: train on two nonconsecutive days, ideally Monday/Thursday or Tuesday/Friday. Keep at least 48 hours between sessions.
  • Session flow: 8–10 minute warm-up, then Blocks 1–3 in order. A round means one set of each exercise in the pair. Complete the two exercises back-to-back, transition for about 15–30 seconds as needed, then take the listed rest after the pair.

Working-set split

In normal weeks each day has 26 exercise-sets: Block 1 = 8, Block 2 = 12, and Block 3 = 6. The lower-body exercises contribute 7 sets on Day 1 (4 squat + 3 leg curl) and 6 sets on Day 2 (3 Romanian deadlift + 3 split squat), for 13 lower sets weekly. The remaining 39 are upper-body sets, so the weekly split is 39/52 upper (75%) and 13/52 lower (25%). Arms count as upper body. Warm-ups are excluded. Deload/consolidation weeks remove one round from every pair, preserving the same slots and yielding 29 upper and 10 lower sets (74.4%/25.6%; 39 total, due to rounding by slot).

Effort and progression

RIR means the number of good-form reps you estimate remain; RPE is approximately 10 − RIR. Stop when another rep would compromise technique. No large compound lift is taken to technical failure.

Use double progression within each phase:

  1. Start at the bottom of the rep range with the target RIR.
  2. Add a rep where possible at the next exposure while keeping the same RIR and clean technique.
  3. Once every working set reaches the top of the range, add the smallest practical load and return toward the bottom.
  4. If you miss the bottom of the range or the target RIR twice, reduce load 5–10% or use the listed substitution.
  5. In weeks 4 and 8, remove one round from every superset and reduce normal-week load by about 10–15%, selecting the load that gives 4 RIR. In week 12, also remove one round from every superset; use 3 RIR and do not chase maximal loads.

Rest longer than listed if needed to achieve the target RIR. Record load, reps, and RIR for each exercise.

Warm-up and session management

  1. Do 4–5 minutes of easy cyclical work, then 3–4 minutes of dynamic movements relevant to the day (bodyweight squat or hinge, shoulder circles, light cable row and press).
  2. Before the first lower and upper compound, perform 2–4 progressively heavier ramp-up sets of 3–8 reps. Keep these easy; they are not working sets and do not count toward the split.
  3. Start a 75-minute timer. Keep equipment transitions close, set up the next pair during rest where courteous, and omit optional extra work rather than rushing prescribed sets.
  4. If the session repeatedly exceeds 75 minutes, use the longest listed rest only for Block 1 and the lower end of the listed rest needs elsewhere, while never shortening rest so far that RIR or technique suffers.

Pattern-matched substitutions

Planned movement Suitable substitution
High-bar or safety-bar squat Hack squat or leg press
Romanian deadlift 45-degree back extension or cable pull-through
Split squat / reverse lunge Single-leg press or step-up
Barbell or dumbbell bench press Chest-press machine or cable press
Shoulder press Landmine press or machine shoulder press
Pull-up / pulldown Any pain-free pulldown grip
Chest-supported or cable row Any supported machine row
Leg curl Seated, lying, or standing leg-curl machine
Cable arm or lateral-raise work Comparable dumbbell or selectorized-machine version

Keep the same rounds, reps, effort, rest, and phase progression when substituting. Choose a nearby station where possible so the superset remains practical. A substitution is a training alternative, not a medical treatment.

Safety rule

Stop any movement that causes sharp, escalating, or unusual pain. Do not push through it; use a pain-free pattern-matched option or end that exercise. Seek appropriate professional clearance for pain, a medical concern, pregnancy, or return after significant injury. Technique quality overrides the rep target.
